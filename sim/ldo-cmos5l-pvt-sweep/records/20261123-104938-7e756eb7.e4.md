Both knobs are testbench-local substitutions on a copy of the design
netlist (`e4_divider_cc.py`): `Cc` is the `w=` token of the
`XCc OUT MZ cap_cmomi` line with `l` held at `30e-6` (the same knob
`run_sweep.sh`'s `subst_cc_width()` turns); `R_div` is the `l=` token
of BOTH `XRtop` and `XRbot`, scaled by the same factor so the divider
ratio -- and with it the regulation setpoint -- is unchanged. Each
substitution fails unless it changes exactly the lines it owns
(`test_e4_divider_cc.py` exercises both negative controls).

Where the divider lives is why one site is not enough: the PSRR
bench instantiates `ldo_core_cmos5l` whole and reads the copy through
its `.include`; the loop-gain bench and the E1 operating-point bench
flatten the core one level and carry their own verbatim `XRtop`/
`XRbot` lines (the E1 bench also a divider replica through which the
divider current is measured), so the same substitution is applied to
the rendered deck's own lines as well. `e4_divider_cc.py patch-deck`
then asserts, per deck, that the `.include` target holds the
overridden lines and that no nominal-valued line survives -- a deck
whose copy moved while its flattened legs stayed nominal is refused
at render time, before anything is submitted.

On the fleet the same fact is re-proven from the committed provenance
(`e4_divider_cc.py` `deck_provenance`, the deck-provenance column of
the checks table): each group's `report.json` lists the design-netlist
copy in its `netlist_closure` with a sha256 equal to a fresh
re-derivation (committed netlist + the point's knobs), the submitted
`tb.spice` includes that copy and carries the bench's own legs at the
point's `l=`, and the corner passed with no error diagnostic.
